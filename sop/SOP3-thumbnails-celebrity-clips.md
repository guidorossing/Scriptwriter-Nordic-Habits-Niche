## Why the thumbnail matters most

The thumbnail decides whether anyone clicks. A great video with a weak thumbnail gets no views. That is why we are strict here, and why it is completely normal to ask the artist for a second, third or fourth version.

**The 3-second test:** look at the thumbnail for 3 seconds on your phone, small, in dark mode. Can you tell who the main person is, and can you tell that something just happened that made everyone else lose it? If not, it is not good enough — no matter how nice it looks on a big screen.

**The one-line version of this whole SOP:** *one famous person keeping a straight face while the people next to them fall apart laughing.* Everything below is detail on how to build that image.

# The 9 rules — what to check on every thumbnail

## Rule 1 — High quality and realistic

Above everything else: the image must be extremely high quality, and the moment we show must look **realistic**. It has to read as a real press photo or a real screenshot — something a journalist could have taken.

This matters more here than on BloopUniverse, because a large part of this niche is fully generated. The moment the viewer thinks "that is AI", they scroll past.

**Check for the usual giveaways:**

- Hands — wrong number of fingers, fused fingers, impossible grip on a glass
- Teeth — too many, too even, melting into the lip
- Ears and jawlines that do not match the person we are selling
- Hairlines that blur into the background
- Background text — signs, posters, name plates that spell nonsense
- Two people lit from two different directions

**Ask yourself:** could this really have happened in that room? If not, ask for a new version.

**Minimum size: 1280 x 720. No exceptions.** All 47 competitor thumbnails we measured deliver a full 1280 x 720 file. Not one is smaller.

## Rule 2 — The title must come back in the thumbnail

Our titles almost always name **one** celebrity:

> "Nobody Can Handle Cillian Murphy's NONCHALANT Personality"
> "No Celebrity Has ZERO Filter Like Matt Damon and It's HILARIOUS"
> "You Won't Believe How SAVAGE Joe Pesci Is"

That means the person in the title must be **the anchor in the middle of the frame, and instantly recognizable.** Not a lookalike, not a bad angle, not half turned away.

**This is where the competition loses money.** A Papa Ruzz video titled "Ozzy Osbourne's MOST UNHINGED Encounters" used a thumbnail of a man who is not recognizably Ozzy. It got 4,500 views on a channel that regularly does 300,000+.

If the title promises that the person has *zero filter*, the thumbnail must show the moment right after they said something. If the title promises they *broke everyone*, the people around them must genuinely be falling apart — not smiling politely.

## Rule 3 — Best of all: a real scene, perfected

The best thing you can have is a thumbnail built from a **real scene** — a real talk show frame, a real press junket, a real award show.

Papa Ruzz proves this: they mostly use unedited screenshots, and their best real-scene thumbnail (Matt Damon deadpan behind the SNL Weekend Update desk while Jennifer Lawrence howls next to him) did 1.5 million views with zero editing.

So the artist should first hunt for a real frame that already contains the deadpan-plus-reaction. Only when that does not exist do we build it — with Format 1, 2 or 3 further down.

**Where to hunt for real frames:** SNL Weekend Update, Graham Norton, Hot Ones, Jimmy Kimmel / Fallon couch segments, press junket round tables, award show audience cutaways, movie blooper reels.

## Rule 4 — 2 or 3 people, never more

In all 47 competitor thumbnails we studied, the foreground is **always 2 or 3 people**. Never one alone, never four or more.

- **3 people = the reaction sandwich.** The title celebrity sits in the middle with a straight face. Left and right, someone is losing it. This is the strongest layout in the niche.
- **2 people = the pair.** The anchor sits on the left, the reaction on the right.

**Why never one person:** a single face has nothing to react to. The click comes from the *contrast* between the calm one and the breaking one — the viewer wants to know what was just said.

**Why never four or more:** the intention gets lost and the faces get too small to read on a phone.

Background crowds are fine (an award show audience), as long as the foreground is still 2 or 3 clear people.

## Rule 5 — Facial expressions: the deadpan anchor (the most important rule)

This is the single most important thing we check.

**Exactly one person is deadpan.** Neutral, bored, unbothered, or quietly amused. That is the title celebrity, and they sit in the middle (with 3 people) or on the left (with 2).

**Everyone else is extreme.** Mouth wide open laughing, doubled over, hand over the mouth, head thrown back, or mid spit-take.

**Never a smile.** A polite smile does not read at 200 pixels wide. It has to be an extreme reaction or nothing.

**Never two identical expressions.** If both people are laughing the same way, the image is dead. The contrast is what earns the click.

**Ask yourself:** can you read both emotions from across the room, with the image the size of a stamp?

## Rule 6 — Brightness and colour

We measured every recent thumbnail on all three reference channels. Despite three completely different production methods, they land on nearly identical colour values. There is one shared house grade behind this niche, and these are the numbers:

| Measurement | Target | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Warm pixels | **0.60** | Skin tones, wood, stage light. Cold blue frames are avoided |
| Saturation | **~115 of 255** | Lifted, but not neon |
| Brightness | **~115 of 255** | Deliberately mid-range. **Never blown-out white** |
| Contrast | **48–62** | Real depth between subject and background |

**This is different from BloopUniverse.** There we push brightness as far as it goes. Here, *warmth* does the standing-out work, not raw brightness. A mid-bright warm frame beats a bright cold one in this niche.

**In practice for the artist:**

- Faces brightly lit, background about one stop darker
- Warm colour cast overall — avoid cold blue and grey scenes
- Strong separation between the subject and the background
- Never grey or washed out

## Rule 7 — No text. At all.

**Default: the thumbnail carries zero words.**

Of the 47 competitor thumbnails we studied, **44 have no text whatsoever.** No title, no caption, no watermark, no logo, no channel signature, no border. Two of the three reference channels have never used text on a single thumbnail.

The reason is Rule 1. The whole format sells "this really happened". A graphic overlay is the one thing that instantly says "this is a made thumbnail", and it breaks the illusion.

**If you do use text — and this should be rare — follow this exact pattern:**

Papa Ruzz is the only reference channel that uses text, on roughly 1 in 6 thumbnails. Their best one did 1.3 million views. The pattern:

- **One quote, 3 to 4 words**, inside quotation marks — e.g. `"he gave me pills"`
- It is a **claim about what was just said**, never a restatement of the title
- **White bold with a black outline.** Not yellow — yellow is the BloopUniverse look
- Placed in an **empty corner**, never over a face
- Lowercase, not ALL CAPS

**Never do all of these at once:** no text *and* a red circle *and* a heavy border. Pick at most one intervention, or preferably zero.

## Rule 8 — Build from the asset library, and do not give up on AI tools

**The competition is not making these one at a time.** We proved that the three reference channels share a production pipeline:

- The **same award-show template** appears 4 times across all 3 channels — identical camera angle, identical hall lighting, identical microphone. Only the celebrity and their costume change. Those four videos did 435K, 672K, 818K and 1.0M.
- The **same Italian restaurant set** (red checkered tablecloths) appears 3 times across 2 channels. We compared the backgrounds pixel by pixel to confirm it.
- The **same balloon prop** appears on two different channels.

So the artist should not start from a blank canvas every week. They should build and reuse:

1. **5–8 backgrounds** that say "this is real": a talk show couch with a night skyline, a press junket room with curtains, a restaurant, an award show audience, a sitcom living room
2. **A set of reaction poses**: the spit-take, the double-over, the hand-over-mouth, the head-back laugh. These are not celebrity-specific — we swap the face and the clothes

**On AI tools and celebrity likeness:** the tools will often refuse or mangle a well-known face, and copyright filters get triggered. **Do not give up straight away.** Try a different angle, a different prompt, a costume instead of a face-on shot, or start from a real frame and edit. If the image truly cannot be made, switch to a different format. What we cannot do is settle for a mediocre image.

## Rule 9 — Red circle or red arrow (last resort only)

If something in the frame really cannot be made to stand out, we can place a **red circle** or a **red arrow** on it.

Papa Ruzz does this occasionally — a hand-drawn red circle around one face.

**Please only use this as the very last option.** It is the second-most obvious "this is a made thumbnail" signal after text.

# Casting — the decision that matters most

**Format sets the floor. The celebrity sets the ceiling.**

This is the clearest pattern in the data. Every format in this niche spans three orders of magnitude — the same spit-take composite produced both 8,800 views and 2.5 million. What separates them is almost never the craft. It is who is in the picture.

The three worst-performing thumbnails we found were all technically fine. They failed on casting:

| Video | Views | Why it failed |
|---|---|---|
| Dick Van Dyke | 2,400 | Beautifully built AI scene. Almost nobody is searching for him |
| Willem Dafoe | 17,000 | Clear, recognizable, well lit. Low demand |
| Steve Carell (solo) | 10,000 | Strong spit-take composite. Solo names underperform ensembles |

## What to cast

**Ensembles from huge franchises beat solo celebrities.**
Big Bang Theory cast 1.5M · Spider-Man trio 2.4M · Avengers 623K · Friends 54K–891K

**Duos with a known relationship beat singles.**
Emily Blunt & John Krasinski 2.5M · Tom Holland & Zendaya 891K · Chris Tucker & Jackie Chan 746K

**Deadpan-coded actors are gold, because the format needs a believable straight face.**
Cillian Murphy 2.5M · Sean Connery 790K · Joe Pesci 672K · Christian Bale 240K · Harrison Ford

**Names with current search demand.** Morgan Freeman 2.4M · Matt Damon 1.5M · Emilia Clarke 1.0M · Ryan Reynolds 1.0M

## What to avoid

- Solo older names with low current demand (Dick Van Dyke, Leslie Nielsen 41K, Phil Collins 93K)
- Anyone whose face is not instantly recognizable at phone size
- A celebrity the artist cannot render convincingly — a bad likeness is worse than a different celebrity

**Honest caveat:** this list comes from 47 thumbnails on three channels, and views are not the same as click-through rate. A strong title also moves the number. Treat it as a starting shortlist, not a law — and add to it from the weekly analytics report (SOP 6).

# Thumbnail checklist

Go through this list for every thumbnail you receive. If you have to answer "no" anywhere, ask for a new version.

**The basics**

☐ The image quality is high — sharp, not blurry, not pixelated, at least 1280 x 720

☐ The scene looks realistic. It could pass as a real press photo

☐ No AI giveaways — check hands, teeth, ears, hairlines, and any text in the background

☐ No weird edit mistakes — no floating limbs, no melted faces, no mismatched lighting

**The message**

☐ The person from the title is in the thumbnail and **instantly recognizable**

☐ You can understand what is happening in 3 seconds, without reading anything

☐ There are 2 or 3 people in the foreground — not 1, not 4 or more

☐ **Exactly one person is deadpan**, and they are the person from the title

☐ The others are extreme — laughing hard, doubled over, or mid spit-take. Not smiling

☐ The deadpan anchor is centred (3 people) or on the left (2 people)

**How it stands out**

☐ Warm colour cast — not cold blue, not grey, not washed out

☐ Faces brightly lit, background about one stop darker

☐ Mid-bright overall — not blown-out white

☐ Faces are big enough to read on a phone screen

☐ Looked at it on a phone, small, in dark mode — and it still works

**The text**

☐ **There is no text.** No words, no logo, no watermark, no border

☐ *If* text was used: one quote, 3–4 words, in quotation marks, white with a black outline, in an empty corner, not over a face

☐ It is **not** yellow (that is the BloopUniverse look)

**Final check**

☐ No red circle or red arrow, unless it truly was the last option

☐ No other channel's watermark visible

☐ Bottom-right corner is clear for YouTube's duration stamp

☐ It is a different format than the last 2 or 3 thumbnails we published

# The 5 thumbnail formats

**Important:** always try a real scene first (Rule 3). Only build something new when a real frame will not work.

## Format 1 — The press-junket row (the reaction sandwich)

**The format in one line:** three people seated in a row against a hotel-suite or junket background, the title celebrity dead centre with a completely blank face, the two beside them falling apart.

This is the highest-volume format in the niche and produced the biggest single winner we found (2.5M).

**Required elements**

- Three people, seated, shoulders roughly level, evenly spaced
- The title celebrity **exactly in the middle**, hands folded or still, face blank
- Left and right: mouth-open laughing, head back, or hand to the chest
- Background: curtains, a hotel suite, plants, soft warm lamps — lightly blurred
- Chest-up crop, faces on the upper third
- **Zero text**

**Best examples:** [Cillian Murphy 2.5M](https://youtu.be/URnPmZbJTG4) · [Christian Bale 240K](https://youtu.be/sIFGcwok8ZU)

## Format 2 — The award-show audience

**The format in one line:** the celebrity sits in an award-show audience, often in costume, holding a microphone, while the entire hall around them laughs.

**This is the most reliable format we found.** It appears four times across all three reference channels, and the *worst* of those four beat the median of every other format:

| Channel | Celebrity | Views |
|---|---|---|
| Celebs Goat | Emilia Clarke | 1.0M |
| Celebs Goat | Matt Damon | 818K |
| Belmorra | Joe Pesci | 672K |
| Papa Ruzz | Dolly Parton | 435K |

**Required elements**

- Formal award-show hall: warm lighting, gold and red tones, a packed crowd behind
- The celebrity seated in the audience, **holding a microphone**
- In costume or signature look where possible (gladiator armour, a stage outfit) — this drives instant recognition
- Recognizable faces in the rows behind, all laughing
- The celebrity's own expression: deadpan, smug, or mid-punchline
- **Zero text**

**Best example:** [Joe Pesci 672K](https://youtu.be/loHAsIVYFTA)

## Format 3 — The spit-take composite

**The format in one line:** two or three recognizable actors, cut out and composited, with at least one mid-spit-take. The spray *is* the brand.

This is the Celebs Goat house format — 13 of their 19 thumbnails.

**Required elements**

- Visible drink spray from at least one mouth — white droplets, frozen mid-motion
- At least one **glass in frame**, held in hand (water glass, champagne flute, tumbler)
- 2 or 3 people, cut out and layered, shoulders slightly overlapping
- A recognizable set from the relevant film or series, lightly blurred
- Costume or character look wherever possible
- **Zero text**

**Placement:** the person spitting goes **left or right, never dead centre.** The centre belongs to the deadpan anchor.

**Variant — the gag composite.** Same build, but instead of a spit-take, a visual joke: an absurd prop, a costume failure, an animal. This variant produced the best numbers in our sample (median 1.0M), but only on three videos — too small to steer on. Worth testing under Format 5.

**Best examples:** [Emily Blunt & Krasinski 2.5M](https://youtu.be/r2opRhXBuAQ) · [Big Bang Theory 1.5M](https://youtu.be/cGhjF-iquuY)

## Format 4 — The real scene, perfected

**The format in one line:** an unedited or barely edited frame from real footage, where the deadpan-plus-reaction already exists.

Cheapest format to produce, and it delivered 1.5M for Papa Ruzz with essentially no editing.

**How to work it**

- Hunt the frame first: SNL Weekend Update, Graham Norton, junket round tables, blooper reels
- Look specifically for one person holding a straight face next to someone losing it
- Then perfect it: sharpen, warm the grade, lift the faces, darken the background
- Do **not** add a border, a logo or text

**Best example:** [Matt Damon 1.5M](https://youtu.be/4HwJffrRbBc)

## Format 5 — Try something new

Roughly **1 in every 5 to 10 thumbnails**, try something completely new. This is how we find the next format that works.

Two things worth testing first, because the data hints at them but cannot prove them:

1. **The gag composite** (Format 3 variant) — best median in our sample, only 3 data points
2. **The Papa Ruzz quote overlay** — one 3–4 word quote in white, no yellow. Their 1.3M video used it

Keep an eye on which of these new attempts get a high CTR, and report it in the weekly analytics report (SOP 6).

# FOR VA: Feedback template for the thumbnail artist

> Hi [name], thanks for the thumbnail for **[video title]**.
>
> It is not there yet, and this is why:
>
> 1. **[what is wrong]** — for example: I cannot tell that the person in the middle is [celebrity]. The title is about them, so they have to be instantly recognizable. Can you use a more front-on angle?
> 2. **[what is wrong]** — for example: everyone is laughing. I need exactly one person keeping a completely straight face — that has to be [celebrity], in the middle — and the other two falling apart around them.
> 3. **[what is wrong]** — for example: there is text on it. This channel uses **no text at all**. Please remove it. (That is the BloopUniverse style, not this one.)
> 4. **[what is wrong]** — for example: the image is cold and grey. Can you warm the grade up and light the faces more, keeping the background a stop darker?
>
> Can you try **format [1 / 2 / 3 / 4]** from our SOP, and start from a real scene if possible?
>
> [Good thing you noticed about this version.] Can you send version 2 before [day + time]?

**Remember:** it is normal for a thumbnail to need 2 or 3 rounds. If it is still not good after 3 rounds, message Guido instead of approving something mediocre.

## Where these numbers come from

Every figure in this SOP comes from a teardown of 47 thumbnails across the three reference channels, done on 16 September 2026: 35 recent uploads plus 12 extra videos picked for extreme performance, to check whether winners and losers follow a different format. Colour, contrast and luminance were measured directly; backgrounds were compared pixel by pixel to prove the shared templates.

**Two honest limits.** Views are not click-through rate — from the outside, a weak thumbnail carried by a strong title looks the same as a real winner. And 47 thumbnails on three channels is a small sample, so treat the numbers as direction, not proof. Re-check the reference channels on "Popular" every few weeks and update this SOP when the format moves.
